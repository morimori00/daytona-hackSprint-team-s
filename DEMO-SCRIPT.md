# Preview Dog — 2 minute walkthrough

About 260 spoken words. Founder voice: you are showing a friend around your
product, not reading a report. Talk to the camera, use your hands, and let the
screen do the work when a video is playing.

Route: **problem → solution → dashboard → a real repository.**

The `…` marks are places to breathe. They matter more than the words.

---

## 0:00 – 0:20 · The problem

**Show:** a GitHub issue. Just text. No proof of anything.

> Let me show you something that kept happening to us.
>
> Someone files a bug. You read it, you try it… and it works fine on your
> machine.
> So before you fix anything, you lose an hour just trying to *see* the bug.
>
> And it goes the other way too.
> A pull request says it adds a feature. To really believe that, you have to
> pull the branch and run it yourself.
>
> We got tired of this.

---

## 0:20 – 0:45 · The solution

**Show:** the landing page. Scroll slowly through the three steps.

> So we built Preview Dog.
>
> You connect your GitHub repo once.
> After that, you just add one label — to an issue, or to a pull request.
> That's the whole workflow.
>
> Behind that label, we start a Daytona sandbox. It boots in under a second.
> We clone your code, start your app, and a browser agent walks through what the
> report actually says — recording the whole time.
>
> Then the video goes straight back into the thread.

---

## 0:45 – 1:20 · The dashboard

**Show:** click Sign in. Go through WorkOS. Land on the dashboard.

> Let me sign in and show you the dashboard.
> We use WorkOS for login, so this is something your whole team can actually
> use.

**Show:** the four numbers at the top.

> Up here you see everything at a glance.
> How many runs we've done… and how many reported bugs were actually real.

**Show:** scroll the table. Point at a green row, then a purple one.

> Down here is every run.
> Green means we reproduced the bug. Purple is a pull request preview.
> And every row has the video.

**Show:** open the assistant. Type `Which runs failed?` and let it stream.

> And if you don't feel like reading a table — just ask.
> It only answers from your real runs. It won't make anything up.

## 1:20 – 1:52 · A real repository

**Show:** the CRM repository on GitHub, then its pull request.

> Now, I know what you're thinking. Sure, it works on *your* app.
>
> So let's look at a real one.
> This is a CRM dashboard. Next.js, ninety-eight dependencies.
> We didn't build it for this demo.

> Same label. Same flow.

**Show:** scroll to the Preview comment and play the recording.

> Preview Dog opened it, clicked through every card, and recorded nineteen

**Do:** stop talking. Let it play for three or four seconds.

> Nobody pulled this branch. Nobody installed anything.
> The reviewer just watched.

**Show:** the landing page.

> That's Preview Dog.

## Notes for the speaker

**The two words to practise.** Both are on screen anyway, so a stumble is fine:

- *Reproducibility* — **re-pro-du-ci-BI-li-ty**. Or just say "the label" and
  point at it. A founder pointing at the screen looks confident, not lost.
- *Daytona* — **day-TOE-na**.

**Say "we", not "the system".** "We start a sandbox" sounds like a founder.
"The system starts a sandbox" sounds like documentation.

**Your numbers are the memorable part.** Under a second. Ninety-eight
dependencies. Nineteen steps. Slow down slightly on each one.

**Never read the comment out loud.** It's already on screen. Silence while a
recording plays is confidence — filling it is nerves.

**Open every tab before you start.** Landing page, dashboard, CRM pull request.
Switching tabs is faster than loading pages, and it never fails live.

**If you're running long,** cut the assistant demo. Everything else carries the
story on its own.

**If something breaks live,** say: "Here's one from earlier" and open an
existing thread. Every run is already on GitHub — you can't actually lose this
demo.
